Last Wednesday June 24, 2026 two earthquakes of 7.2. and 7.5 on Richter scale hit Venezuela in less than a minute. At 18:04:33 and 18:05:11 respectively.

Geologists call this a "doublet". It’s defined as two major quakes close in time.

We’ve had earthquakes before but none like this.

Entire buildings collapsed. Families torn apart. Kids, pets, men, women buried under concrete. La Guaira became one of the most affected areas of loss. Over 100 residential and tourist buildings collapsed entirely. And many people with them.

At the time of writing this, the numbers are still moving, which is the worst part cause people are still being rescued. As of June 27, the official toll sits near 920 dead and over 3,300 injured. The UN estimates more than 50,000 people are still missing under the rubble. Citizen-made lists circulating on social media name over 52,000. Still everyone is affected directly or indirectly by this.

### Supporting From the Distance

I left Venezuela five years ago, I built a different reality, in another country but when something like this happens you realize the migratory grief was always there, latent, underneath.

Thankfully, my family is safe since they live in a state outside the worst-hit zones. Still I can’t feel relieve because of seeing other people suffer, as that suffering becomes mine too.

I saw colleagues and friends of mine driving from one state to another carrying supplies (water, food, clothes, medicine), showing up to hospitals already overwhelmed, rescue workers and regular neighbors digging through collapsed concrete with their bare hands and tools to rescue people.

Several businesses and companies gathering supplies, giving free rides, and gathering funds (like [Yummy](https://dona.yummyrides.com/)) to aid the victims.

All of this made me feel really proud and moved. It’s the reason I’m writing this. To showcase the best part of Venezuela: their people.

Other countries showed up too. More than 17 countries sent aid. Over 30 international search-and-rescue teams with hundreds of specialists deployed on the ground, like El Salvador’s, Madrid’s own ERICAM, American rescuers, and many others giving a hand to a catastrophe this country was never prepared for.

### Why the consequences are worse than they should be

Simply: because of twenty-six years of Chavismo.

Hospitals were already damaged and under-resourced _**before**_ the earthquake.

More than 90% of households reported chronic failures in basic water and electricity _**before**_ June 24.

Nearly 8 million people already needed humanitarian assistance _**before**_ a single building fell.

Fire departments, police, public infrastructure, building codes, roads, systematically downgraded on purpose. Gutting the country’s capacity to protect itself by stealing the money to enrich themselves.

This is the worst tragedy of all the daily tragedies these 26 years have produced. All made on purpose. There’s enough material for a series of articles, movies, and series of Netflix on how the regime destroyed our nation, but the goal of this post is to highlight what the citizens in and out of Venezuela are doing right at this very moment, and how to actually help wherever you are reading this.

### How to Help From Anywhere

- **Deliver supplies in your city:** Look for Venezuelan collection points (centros de acopio) in your area this might be churches, community organizations, warehouses. Diaspora groups are coordinating shipments. What's most needed: water, non-perishable food, hygiene kits, blankets, flashlights, batteries, first aid supplies, medications, USEFUL clothes. Check this web to locate centers in and out of Venezuela: [Donation centers for Venezuela](https://centrosayudavenezuela.org/)

- **Donate money**: in my opinion this is the highest-impact option from abroad, because it lets organizations buy exactly what's needed locally.
